## Full issue body
## Problem

Family sharing is the most heavily built capability in the app and one of the least findable. The only route into `ManageGuardiansScreen` is a `'caregivers'` entry in a popup menu on a profile row in `lib/ui/profiles/profile_picker_screen.dart:77`. There is:

- no Settings entry for sharing,
- no "Shared with me" view listing profiles another guardian shared with you,
- no badge or indicator anywhere that a profile *is* shared, or that an invitation is pending,
- no indication on the main screen that a second guardian exists at all.

This is named as deferred work in `docs/plans/2026-09-06-001-feat-family-sharing-invitations-plan.md`: *"Sharing discoverability. The only route to `ManageGuardiansScreen` is a `'caregivers'` popup action on a profile row... There is no Settings entry, no 'shared with me' view, and no pending-invite badge."*

## Why it matters

Collaboration between guardians is the product's differentiator, and a differentiator hidden behind an unlabeled overflow menu does not differentiate anything. Concretely:

- A parent who wants to add the other parent has no obvious path. The feature they came for is three taps deep behind an icon with no affordance suggesting sharing.
- A guardian who *accepted* an invitation has no view of what was shared with them, separate from what they own. Shared profiles land in the same undifferentiated profile list.
- An invitation sent and never redeemed is invisible until you go looking. Invitations expire (1-168h TTL); a silently expired invite reads to the sender as "the feature is broken".
- After the ownership-transfer flow ships a profile to a young person's own account, the parent's continuing access is an ordinary membership. Nothing in the UI tells them which profiles they now hold by membership rather than ownership.

## Scope

- **Settings entry point.** A "Family & sharing" section in `lib/ui/settings/settings_screen.dart` listing profiles you share or that are shared with you, each routing to its Manage Guardians screen.
- **"Shared with me" grouping.** In the profile picker, visually separate profiles you are the primary guardian of from profiles you hold via membership, with the role shown.
- **Shared indicator.** A profile with more than one accepted guardian shows a small, non-alarming indicator (count or icon) in the picker and near the profile header, so a guardian always knows they are looking at a co-managed record.
- **Pending-invite badge.** When a profile has an outstanding, unredeemed, unexpired invitation, badge it — in the picker, in Settings, and inside Manage Guardians (the pending list already exists inside that screen; this is about surfacing it from outside).
- **Expired-invite state.** An invitation that aged out should be visible and re-sendable, not silently absent.

## Design constraints

- Pending invitations are deliberately **not** synced into the local Drift store (that plan's KTD3: a recipient label plus a minor's profile id is itself sensitive, and invites are short-lived). A badge therefore needs connectivity to be accurate. Design for the offline case honestly — show no badge rather than a stale one, and do not block the screen on the fetch.
- Do not add a new local retention surface for invitation data to make the badge cheaper.
- Respect roles: a `viewer` or `caregiver` sees that a profile is shared but does not see invite/revoke controls, matching the existing role ladder.
- This is navigation and presentation work only. No schema change, no new RPC, no change to the invite lifecycle.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Sharing is reachable from Settings in at most two taps, with a label that says what it is.
- [ ] The profile picker visually distinguishes owned profiles from profiles shared with you, and shows your role on the latter.
- [ ] A co-managed profile is identifiable as co-managed without opening any menu.
- [ ] An outstanding invitation is badged outside Manage Guardians; redeeming or cancelling it clears the badge.
- [ ] With no network, the screens render without a badge and without an error state or a spinner that never resolves.
- [ ] A `viewer` sees the sharing surfaces read-only, with no invite or revoke affordance.
