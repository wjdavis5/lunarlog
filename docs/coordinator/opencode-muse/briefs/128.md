## Full issue body
## Problem

Everything two guardians can record in this app is attached to a single calendar date. The day entry model (`lib/domain/models/day_entry.dart`) holds `flow`, `tags`, and one free-text `note` per date. There is no place for anything that spans dates or belongs to the profile rather than to a day: a standing note about what usually helps, a question to raise at the next appointment, a list of what to bring, a heads-up from one parent to the other.

So the coordination that co-parenting actually requires happens outside the app, in text messages — which is exactly the friction the multi-guardian feature exists to remove. Issue #8 framed the goal as guardians staying "coordinated on supplies and care without needing to text back and forth"; today the app solves the *visibility* half of that and none of the *coordination* half.

## Why it matters

- A clinician visit is the single highest-value moment in this data's life, and it is the moment the app currently serves worst. There is no way to accumulate "things to ask" over the six weeks before an appointment, from both parents, in one place.
- Standing care knowledge has nowhere to live. "Ibuprofen at the first sign, not after" or "she runs out of supplies at her dad's" is not a fact about one Tuesday. Writing it into a day note buries it.
- The young person themselves has no voice in the record beyond a day note. A shared list they can add to is a low-stakes way to give them one.
- This is a genuinely differentiated capability. Partner-sharing in a conventional tracker is a one-way read feed. Two guardians *writing to a shared surface* about the person they both care for is not something a single-user tracker's sharing model does.

## Scope

Per-profile shared content, visible to every accepted guardian on that profile:

- **Care notes**: a small set of standing, non-date-bound notes on the profile. Each carries who wrote it and when, using the same attribution treatment as day entries.
- **Visit prep list**: a checklist of items/questions for an upcoming appointment. Any guardian with write access can add, check off, and remove items. Checked items stay visible until the list is cleared, so the other parent can see what was covered.
- Both surfaces sync through the existing sync engine and RLS model, scoped to the profile, with the same role gating as day entries (`viewer` reads, does not write).
- Reachable from the profile screen, and referenced from wherever export is offered — a clinician-facing export should be able to carry the prep list.

## Design constraints

- **This is health content about a minor.** It syncs and it is readable by every guardian on the profile, which is exactly the point, but it must inherit the full protection posture: covered by the device-credential gate, excluded from crash reports by the existing scrubber allowlist, never included in a notification payload, and length-bounded server-side (see issue #94 for the precedent — unbounded text amplifies to every guardian's device).
- Needs a Drift schema bump and a matching Supabase migration with RLS proved by pgTAP, following the pattern of `profile_guardians`. Do not attempt to smuggle this into an existing table's spare column.
- Keep the model deliberately small. This is not a chat feature, not threaded, and has no notifications of its own — a new item shows up in the shared activity feed if that ships, and nowhere else. Resist scope growth into messaging.
- Concurrent edits by two guardians need a resolution rule stated up front, consistent with the existing conflict work: a checklist of independent items converges naturally (add/remove are commutative on a set); a free-text note does not, so pick and document last-writer-wins or per-note ownership rather than inventing a merge.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A guardian can add a care note and a prep-list item on a shared profile; a second guardian sees both after a sync, attributed correctly.
- [ ] A `viewer` can read both surfaces and cannot write to either, enforced server-side and reflected in the UI.
- [ ] Checking off a prep-list item is visible to the other guardian, including who checked it.
- [ ] Text length is bounded server-side; an over-length write is rejected, not truncated silently.
- [ ] Content never appears in a notification payload or a crash report — proved by a test over the scrubber allowlist, as the existing observability tests do.
- [ ] RLS is proved by pgTAP: a non-guardian reads nothing; a revoked guardian reads nothing.
- [ ] Two guardians adding items concurrently while offline both keep their items after sync.
