LunarLog ("we", "our", or "the app") is a privacy-first menstrual cycle and symptom tracker built for families: an account syncs a profile across a guardian's own devices and lets it be shared with other guardians — co-parents, caregivers — each with their own role, so sync and multi-guardian collaboration are the product's priority, not a bolted-on option. We believe that reproductive and menstrual health data is deeply personal and sensitive. LunarLog is architected from the ground up to protect your privacy: your data is protected at rest by each device's own operating-system encryption (Section 1), nothing you log is uploaded until you sign in, and the app keeps logging, viewing, and predicting even when you have no network connection.

This Privacy Policy explains what information LunarLog processes, how that information is protected, and the choices and rights you have.

## 1. Core Principles

- **Built For Sync & Family Collaboration:** LunarLog's account (Supabase Auth) is what lets a profile sync across a guardian's own devices and be shared with other guardians — co-parents and caregivers each get their own role, invitation, and attribution (Section 5). This is the product's priority and its differentiator, not an add-on; nothing about it changes what data is collected, only that sharing and multi-device sync are what the app is built to do.
- **Protected at Rest:** All data stored on your device relies on your operating system's own at-rest protection (iOS Data Protection / Android device encryption) and is only shown behind device-level biometric or passcode authentication. A signed-in browser build is the deliberate exception: a browser's local storage is not protected by an operating-system app sandbox or a biometric gate, and Section 6 states exactly what a signed-in browser holds. On Android, the local database is excluded from cloud backup and device-to-device transfer by declaration (a database backed up off-device would leave the app's own protections behind). On iOS, the app marks the database file excluded from backup and applies operating-system file protection so it stays unreadable until first device unlock after a restart (matching the session Keychain class) — verified on device before release.
- **No Advertising or Data Brokers:** We do not display advertisements, sell your data, monetize your health information, or share data with data brokers or tracking networks.
- **Resilient Offline:** The app keeps working without a network — logging, viewing history, and predictions all run from the copy already on your device, and nothing you do offline is blocked or degraded. This is a reliability property, not a requirement to avoid an account: LunarLog does not require an internet connection to be useful in the moment, but syncing and sharing a profile with other guardians does require signing in.
- **Estimates From Your Own Logged Data:** LunarLog estimates the timing of the next period from the cycle history you log, computed entirely on your device; a guardian who can see a profile's cycle data can see its estimates. LunarLog also estimates a fertile window and an ovulation day the same way — a standard calendar-method back-calculation (next predicted period start minus an assumed luteal-phase length) computed entirely on your device from the same logged cycle history, carrying the same reliability tier as the period estimate it is derived from, and covered by the same guardian-visibility and ownership-transfer rules as all other cycle data (Section 5). It is not based on any additional signal you log (temperature, cervical mucus, or test results) and **must not be used to prevent pregnancy** — it is not birth control, nor a backup to birth control. An earlier version of this policy guaranteed that no fertility feature would ever exist; that guarantee was removed by owner decision in September 2026, and this fertile-window/ovulation estimate is the first feature built under that removal (see the Change History in Section 10). Conception and pregnancy features remain outside the product's scope.
- **Minimal, Scrubbed Telemetry:** Crash and diagnostic reports are strictly scrubbed on your device before transmission to ensure no health data, personal notes, dates, or user identities ever leave your device.

## 2. Information We Collect and Process

### A. Health & Cycle Information (Local & Optional Cloud Sync)
When you log entries in LunarLog, you may record:
- Dates and timestamps of menstrual cycles.
- The IANA time zone (e.g. `America/New_York`) each entry was logged in — used only to derive which calendar day the entry belongs to. Separately, a guardian who turns on caregiver alerts stores *their own* IANA time zone in their notification preferences, which is what resolves quiet-hours and digest timing; an entry's zone is never used for that. Each is a coarse location signal (it identifies one of roughly 400 world regions, not a precise location) and is stored and synced under the same rules as the entry itself.
- Flow intensity levels (an explicit "not bleeding today" note, light, medium, heavy, super heavy).
- Personal tags and custom notes.
- Individual tracked symptoms and options (e.g. a specific pain, mood, or body symptom; spotting; a numeric measurement like temperature; an intensity rating) — stored as their own records tied to the day they were logged, the same way flow and tags are, and covered by the same storage and sync rules described here.
- Import/device provenance: which entries were logged manually versus imported from a source such as Clue, Apple Health, or Health Connect, and an internal key identifying the specific import run — used to label an entry in the app (e.g. "Imported from Clue") and to avoid creating duplicate entries when the same import is run again. This label is metadata about how an entry was recorded, not a new category of health content, and is covered by the same storage and sync rules as the entry itself.
- Profile information (display name, sort order, archive status, minor status flag).
- Same-date merge disclosures: when two guardians log the same date independently and the app merges the two entries, the value that lost the merge (a note's text, or a flow level) is kept briefly in a disclosure record so the guardian who wrote it can see what happened and recover their text in the app. That record carries the discarded value itself plus which guardians were involved, is visible only to the profile's guardians, never appears in any notification, and is permanently deleted 30 days after the merge (see Section 7) — the one category here that is intentionally short-lived. A data export you run while a disclosure is still inside that window includes it (your own discarded writing is yours to take with you); once the window has passed, neither the app nor any later export holds it.
- Shared change history: when an entry changes, the server keeps a short audit record of THAT a change happened — which guardian made it, when, which fields (e.g. "note", "flow"), and the kind of change (logged, updated, deleted, or discarded by a same-date merge). It deliberately never records what any field SAID: no note text, no tag, no flow level. It is visible only to the profile's guardians (the same access the entries themselves already grant), and each record is permanently deleted 90 days after the change (see Section 7). It is bookkeeping about your entries, not a second copy of them.
- Guardian notes: alongside the shared day note, a guardian can add their **own** dated note about a specific day — one note per guardian per date, attributed to its author, and never merged with another guardian's note for the same day (two guardians writing about the same date produce two separate notes, both kept). A guardian can edit or remove only their own note. A guardian note is health content and is stored and synced under the same rules as the shared day note, except that it is author-scoped: it is never combined with, or overwritten into, anyone else's note. It never appears in a notification payload or in a crash report, and it is removed when the profile is deleted, when the account is deleted, and when the guardian who wrote it is revoked from the profile. Every accepted guardian — including the person the note is about (see Section 5) — can read it, and the app states that audience at the point of writing.

**Storage:** This information is stored directly on your device in a SQLite database protected by your operating system's at-rest protection (see Section 1). Signing in and enabling cloud sync is what moves it to your account, where it syncs across your own devices and to any guardian the profile is shared with; before you sign in, none of it leaves the device.

### B. Account & Authentication Information (Optional)
An account is what makes cross-device sync and sharing a profile with other guardians possible, and creating one remains your choice — a build with no cloud configuration has no account at all. You can log, view, and get predictions in LunarLog without ever creating an account. If you sign in and enable cross-device sync, we process:
- **Email Address:** Used for account verification, passwordless sign-in links, and password recovery.
- **Authentication Credentials:** Handled securely via Supabase Auth. We support email/password, passwordless magic links, Sign in with Apple, and Google Sign-In.
- **Provider Identifiers:** If you authenticate via Apple or Google, we store a cryptographic subject identifier (`sub`) provided by the identity provider to link your account. We do not access your contacts, external profile files, or social graphs.

**Upload Consent:** When you sign in on a device that already holds local data, LunarLog asks for your explicit consent before uploading existing records to your account.

### C. Support Ticket Information (Optional)

If you use "Send feedback" (Settings, signed-in accounts only), we process:
- **The message you write**, your category selection (bug, feature request, support, other), and the reply email address you provide (pre-filled from your account, editable).
- **Diagnostics you approve:** app version, build number, OS name and version, device model, active locale, and up to 25 recent in-app navigation/error breadcrumbs — never health data, account identity, or credentials. The app shows you the exact diagnostics payload and lets you turn it off before you submit.
- **An optional screenshot**, attached only after an explicit consent step naming the risk that a screenshot of this app usually contains cycle data for a family member.

None of this leaves your device until you tap submit. A ticket you send is visible to the app's operator team and to you, in the app's Support history. Submitting a ticket also sends a short alert email to the operator (the ticket id, category, app version, and submission time — not your message, reply email, or attachment). That alert email lives in the operator's own inbox under that inbox's own retention, separate from the database record described above — deleting a ticket or your account (see "Support Ticket Retention" in Section 7) removes the database record, not a copy already delivered by email.

### D. Technical & Crash Information (Diagnostics)
To maintain app stability and diagnose crashes, LunarLog includes optional telemetry powered by Sentry.
- **Strict Privacy Floor:** Before any error or crash report leaves your device, an automated client-side scrubber (`lib/observability/scrub.dart`) removes all health information, dates, flow levels, notes, tags, profile names, user IDs, device names, auth tokens, and request payloads. A crash report may additionally carry the *names of the screens you visited* before the crash (e.g. "Settings", "Feedback") — never their contents or arguments; an unrecognized or third-party screen name is reported as "unknown" rather than passed through.
- **Native crash reports bypass this on-device scrubber.** An Android app freeze, an Android native (NDK) crash, an iOS app hang, or an iOS watchdog termination is assembled and sent by the operating system's crash-reporting layer, not by this app's code — it never passes through the scrubber described above. These reports carry device, thread, and stack state, not app content (no note, tag, date, or profile name is ever held in a form a native crash report can capture), and the same server-side scrubbing and IP suppression covers them.
- **Optional performance measurement:** if the operator running the app has separately enabled it, LunarLog can also measure how long the app takes to start and how long some in-app operations take. When enabled, this carries operation timings and HTTP status codes — never a URL's query string, never a request body, and never anything from the allowlist above. This measurement is off by default in every release build.
- Crash data is anonymous, not linked to your identity or health records, and used solely for bug fixes and app performance.

### E. Home-Screen Widget Data (On-Device Only)
If you add the LunarLog home-screen widget (issue #141), the app writes a small, fixed set of facts into an operating-system shared container (an iOS App Group / an Android widget preferences store) that the widget — and only the widget — can read. This container is deliberately **outside** the encrypted database described in Section 2.A, so what is allowed to cross it is kept to the absolute minimum and documented in the app's source: a coarse state word (`day` or "nothing to show"), the current cycle-day count (e.g. "14"), the days-until-next-period countdown when an estimate is active (a relative count, never a date), whether the quick-log button is offered for you on that profile, an opaque random profile identifier (so the button reaches the right profile; never displayed), and the date the counts are anchored to (so the widget can keep its count current between app uses). The widget never displays your profile's name, a calendar date, a flow level, or any symptom — its default render is deliberately discreet (a bare count on the app's brand background), because a widget renders on your home screen where the app's lock screen and app-switcher protections (the opaque cover and Android's FLAG_SECURE) cannot reach. A widget's quick-log button never writes on its own: tapping it opens the app, and the "period started today" entry is applied only after you unlock the app, exactly once, through the same write path as logging inside the app. If the profile's role would not let you log from inside the app (for example, a viewer on a shared profile), the button is not offered and a tap cannot write. Nothing in the widget container ever leaves your device, is synced, or is included in exports.

## 3. How We Use Your Information

We use the information we process strictly for the following purposes:
1. **Core App Functionality:** Enabling you to log, view, and organize cycle records across profiles.
2. **Cross-Device Synchronization (Optional):** Mirroring your cycle records across your authorized devices using row-level-secured cloud storage, protected in transit and at rest exactly as described in Section 6 — TLS in transit, Supabase's own AES-256 infrastructure encryption at rest, never an app-managed cipher.
3. **App Reliability & Security:** Diagnosing technical faults and preventing unauthorized access through biometric locks and automated inactivity relocking.

We **do not** use your information for targeted advertising, marketing communications, automated profiling, or behavioral tracking.

**HIPAA Non-Applicability Notice:**
LunarLog is a direct-to-consumer software application developed for personal menstrual cycle tracking, family collaboration, and health self-management. LunarLog is **not** a "covered entity" and is **not** a "business associate" of any healthcare provider or health plan under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (HIPAA) or its implementing regulations (45 C.F.R. Parts 160 and 164). Information stored on your device or synchronized through LunarLog's cloud features is not Protected Health Information (PHI) governed by HIPAA rules.
Any clinical data exports generated through the app (such as the JSON "Export my data", FHIR R4 clinical summary bundles, or CSV tabular exports described in Section 7) are user-directed, user-controlled transfers of personal records directly to individuals, clinicians, or health portals chosen entirely by you. Generating an export or handing it to a healthcare provider does not establish a HIPAA business associate relationship or healthcare-provider relationship between LunarLog and that provider or healthcare organization.

**On-device health-platform sync (Apple Health / Health Connect) is not an off-device transfer.** With your explicit opt-in (Settings, one bound profile), LunarLog can write the period, flow, intermenstrual-bleeding, tracked symptom categories (such as abdominal cramps, headache, bloating, acne, fatigue, nausea, appetite changes, and sleep changes), mood changes (written to Apple Health on iOS as generic 'Mood Changes' symptom entries without preserving specific emotional valence), cervical-mucus, ovulation-test, and basal-body-temperature records you log in the app into your device's own health store — Apple Health on iOS, Health Connect on Android (symptom and mood writes are currently supported on iOS only) — so other health apps you choose can see the same history. On iOS, LunarLog can also **read menstrual flow** from Apple Health, and on Android it can **read menstrual flow and intermenstrual-bleeding records** from Health Connect, but only when you explicitly start an import (Settings → Health app sync → Import from Apple Health, or Import from Health Connect on Android) and only for the one profile bound to this device: the menstrual-flow and intermenstrual-bleeding records the store returns for the whole available history — read a page at a time, however far back your data goes, not a fixed recent window — are added to that profile's day entries and spotting observations on this device and labelled as imported from Apple Health or Health Connect, and a value you logged by hand is never overwritten. Intermenstrual-bleeding records are stored as spotting observations, the same shape the app logs spotting in. Samples this app itself wrote are excluded by source filtering — HealthKit's recording source, Health Connect's `dataOrigin` — so syncing out and importing back does not duplicate entries. A basal-body-temperature reading is written only when you logged it yourself (or imported it from another tracker); a reading marked as coming from a wearable or another health platform is never written back and is never merged with a reading you logged by hand. The fertility writes are write-only: LunarLog does not read cervical-mucus, ovulation-test, or basal-body-temperature records back from either platform. Reading is user-initiated and on-demand only — there is no automatic or background import. Apple Health does not tell apps whether read access was allowed, and Health Connect's own denied-read signal is treated the same way, so an empty result is treated identically to a denied one and is never described as either. The import reads only user-recorded menstrual flow and intermenstrual bleeding. Separately and on iOS only, when you run that import LunarLog also **reads Apple's four computed cycle-deviation types** (irregular cycles, infrequent cycles, prolonged periods, and persistent intermenstrual bleeding) over a bounded six-month window and, if any are present, shows them on the overview as a clearly labelled, dismissible second opinion sitting next to — never merged into — lunarlog's own estimate, because Apple computes them from whatever was logged in Apple Health and may disagree with lunarlog's history. These four types are **read-only, permanently**: LunarLog never writes them back, its write surface has no method that accepts one, and LunarLog never writes a predicted or derived value (a next-period prediction, a fertile-window estimate, an ovulation estimate) into the health store (App Store Review guideline 5.1.3). All of this stays on your device: LunarLog does not transmit health data anywhere as part of this feature, and no third party receives it through it. The App Store Connect privacy details and Google Play Data safety declarations that cover the read capability are updated separately (issue #21); the Health disclosure above covers the optional Supabase cloud sync of these entries, which is a separate, independently disclosed flow.

**Clinical export is a user-initiated transfer, not collection.** The exports described in Section 7 ("Export my data", JSON; "Export clinical summary", FHIR; "Export as CSV", CSV) are built entirely on your device and handed to your operating system's own share sheet — you pick the destination (AirDrop, Files, email, another app) and only that destination receives a copy. That is a transfer you initiate and control, not a transmission LunarLog performs to a server of ours or a third party's, and it is not app-side collection of a new data type for store-declaration purposes.

## 5. Minors' Privacy & Family Profiles

LunarLog is intended to be operated by a parent, legal guardian, or adult individual. 
- **Minimum-Age Policy (13+ to create an account alone; under 13 only through a parent's invitation):** LunarLog is licensed for and intended to be used by individuals aged 13 and older, or by parents and legal guardians creating and managing profiles for household members. An individual aged 13 or older may create an account and use the app directly. An individual under 13 may not sign up on her own; she may use the app on her own device only when a parent or legal guardian has created her profile and invited her to it through the app's "her own profile" invitation (Section 5, "Her Own Profile"), and accepting that invitation is the record of the parent's or guardian's consent for her to use the app. Primary account management or paid services require individuals to be 18 or older. We do not knowingly collect personal data directly from children under 13 without verifiable parental or guardian authorization and supervision. An explicit acknowledgement of this standard is required during onboarding: an operator who arrived on their own acknowledges the 13-or-older statement, while an operator arriving through a parent's or guardian's invitation acknowledges that the parent or guardian created the profile and invited her, not that she is 13 or older. When you are signed in, that acknowledgement is recorded on your account — with how you arrived (your own acknowledgement, or a parent's or guardian's invitation), when it was made, the app version that made it, and the policy version you acknowledged — included in "Export my data", and removed when you delete your account; when the policy version changes, you are asked to acknowledge it again.
- **How Minor Status Is Determined:** A profile's minor status is **derived from the profile's birth year when one is present** — a profile is a minor until they are 18, and because birth year is a year only with no day or month, the comparison is deliberately conservative, so a profile born late in the year they turn 18 still counts as a minor through that calendar year — and falls back to the stored "this is a minor" flag for a profile with no birth year. It is set by whoever creates or manages the profile, and it gates the minor-specific protections described in this section — most notably the prediction-only sharing refusal below, and the app's default-hidden symptom and mood tracking categories.
- **Guardian Roles & Read Visibility:** While write capabilities are governed by guardian role (a `viewer` cannot log or edit entries, and only primary guardians or co-parents can edit profile settings), every accepted guardian on a profile — whatever the role, and including a parent who created the profile — reads everything the subject logs (cycle entries, symptoms, observations, tags, and shared notes), with one deliberate exception: a day note the subject herself marked **private**, which only she can read. That privacy choice is made when the note is written — a note that has already been saved and shared cannot be made private retroactively — and the server enforces it, not only the app: a private note's text is never sent to a guardian's device, and the app shows a "Private note" placeholder in its place rather than the note editor.
- **Two-stage custodian model:** A profile starts under the adult who created it, who holds both `profiles.user_id` (the account that can delete the record) and the profile's primary-guardian membership. That parent can later **transfer ownership** of a minor's profile to the minor's own account once they are ready to hold it themselves. Transferring moves both facts together to the child's account in a single operation and stamps the profile with when the move happened; every past entry keeps its original "logged by" attribution unchanged, so the record's history is never rewritten. The parent chooses, at the moment they initiate the transfer, whether they keep logging as a co-manager or drop to read-only; either way the parent's continued access afterward is an ordinary membership, not custodial control — the child can revoke it at any time, the same way any guardian can be removed from a shared profile. A parent who deletes their own account no longer removes a profile they have transferred away; conversely, if the child later deletes their own account, the profile and its history go with it, exactly as they would for any account holder.
- **Her Own Profile (subject membership):** A minor (or any profile subject) can hold her own membership on the profile a parent created, alongside the guardians, without waiting for an ownership transfer. A parent sends a "her own profile" invitation; when she accepts, her device lists the profile under "My profile" — never as a shared profile she merely helps with — and her own logging is attributed to her name in the day sheet and the activity feed, exactly like any guardian's. The membership grants ordinary logging access (the same permissions as a caregiver: she can log days, but cannot remove a guardian, edit the profile's details, or delete it — enforced by the server, not just the app), and the guardians already on the profile can still see and log it too, openly and symmetrically, in both directions. This is a labelling and identity fact about the membership, not a new kind of access: the role ladder, every sharing rule in this policy, and the two-stage custodian model above all apply to a subject membership unchanged, and a full ownership transfer (with deletion rights) remains the separate, later decision it always was.
- **No Direct Marketing or Tracking:** We do not knowingly collect personal data directly from children under 13 (or under 16 in certain jurisdictions) without parental consent. Minor profiles receive exactly the same protections as adult profiles — the same Row-Level Security, the same TLS-in-transit and AES-256-at-rest infrastructure protection (Section 6, not an app-managed or end-to-end cipher) — and are never shared or analyzed.
- **Guardian Notes Are Visible To The Child:** A note a parent or other guardian writes about a minor's day is not private to the adult who wrote it. Under the owner's open-and-transparent decision (issue #800), every accepted guardian on the profile can read every guardian note — **including the minor herself when she holds an account on her own profile** — while only the note's own author can edit or remove it and only a guardian who can already log entries on the profile can write one (a `viewer` cannot). The app states that audience on the writing surface itself, not only in this policy. This is deliberately different from a general-purpose private journal: LunarLog does not offer a hidden note about a child.
- **Prediction-Only Sharing Excludes Minors:** A profile's primary guardian can create a *prediction-only connection*: a single-use code that lets exactly one other LunarLog account see that profile's estimated period, fertile, ovulation, and PMS days on a read-only calendar — never a logged entry, note, symptom, or flow value, and never guardian access of any kind. The snapshot is computed on the sharing guardian's own device from the profile's logged history, uploaded only while the connection is active, and deleted from our servers the moment the connection is revoked or the account or profile is deleted. Because minor profiles are never shared, this connection cannot be created for a minor's profile, and a code already issued stops working if the profile is marked as a minor's before it is redeemed — the server refuses both, not just the app. The connection is revocable by either side: the sharer (or the profile's current primary guardian) can end it, and the recipient can stop receiving it themselves at any time — either action deletes the shared snapshot from our servers immediately.
- **Fertility Data Follows Cycle Data:** The fertile-window/ovulation estimate, and any fertility-related data added later, receive no special-case handling of any kind: a guardian who can already see a profile's cycle data sees that profile's fertility estimate too, and it transfers with the profile in an ownership transfer exactly like every other entry on it. The same feature set is available on every profile, including minor profiles, with no restriction — an explicit owner decision (September 2026), not a default.

## 6. Security Protections

We implement rigorous technical safeguards to ensure the security and confidentiality of your data:
- **Device Encryption:** The local database relies on your device's own operating-system-level encryption (iOS Data Protection / Android device encryption), shown to you only after biometric or passcode authentication.
- **Signed-In Browser Build (Web):** The web build is a first-class client only when the operator compiled it with `LUNARLOG_WEB_SYNC=true`; on any other web build accounts and sync do not run. When that build is signed in, two things live in the browser's own storage rather than an operating system's protected store: the profiles and day entries synced to it (in the browser's IndexedDB store, with no app-managed cipher — the same non-end-to-end posture the "Cloud Storage Encryption (not end-to-end)" bullet below already states for the cloud copy), and the session itself (an access token, a refresh token, and the PKCE verifier, readable by any script running on the app's origin). Unlike the iOS and Android builds, this browser copy is **not** protected by device encryption or a biometric/passcode lock, and a browser extension or injected script with access to the page can read it. The app mitigates what it can without a trusted server component: a strict Content Security Policy and cross-origin isolation are served with the build, so only the app's own same-origin code runs and no third-party CDN scripts are loaded. One narrow exception to "same-origin only": when text contains a character the bundled fonts lack — an emoji, or a name in a non-Latin script — the browser may fetch the needed fallback font file from Google Fonts (`fonts.gstatic.com`). That request carries the browser's IP address and never any entry content; the policy permits that host only for fonts. The server-side authorization model is unchanged — every request still carries your session and is still governed by Row-Level Security, so a browser session can reach only the rows your guardian memberships entitled you to see (the "Database Row-Level Security" bullet below applies exactly as it does on a phone); the residual risk a browser adds is theft of the session by code running in the page, not broader access. Signing out — or the banner's "Wipe local data" action — removes the browser's copy of the synced data and the session; clearing the browser's site data does the same without contacting your account. A build with sync off holds no session and no synced rows at all.
- **Backup Exclusion:** On Android, the local database is excluded from both cloud backup and device-to-device transfer by declaration. On iOS, the app marks the database file excluded from backup and applies file protection so it is unreadable until the first unlock after a restart (matching the session Keychain class) — verified on device before release.
- **Home-Screen Widget Container:** The optional home-screen widget reads a small operating-system shared container (Section 2.E) outside the encrypted database. Only the six documented fields above may be written there, the widget's render is discreet by design, and — because a widget draws on the launcher's own surface, outside the windows the app-switcher snapshot suppression (an opaque cover plus Android's FLAG_SECURE) protects — discretion is the privacy control for that surface: the widget never carries content the lock screen itself could not show.
- **Biometric Security:** Biometric authentication (Face ID / Touch ID / Android Biometrics) required to unlock the app.
- **Optional In-App PIN:** You can additionally require a numeric PIN, set in Settings, as a second lock layer on top of your device credential — useful on a household device also used by someone else. It never replaces the device credential by default; it is an additive layer you opt into. The PIN is never sent to our servers or synced between your devices: only a salted, computationally slow hash of it is kept, in the same device-local secure storage (Keychain/Keystore) as your device credential's own state, never in the local database and never as part of any export. Repeated wrong guesses are locked out for a period that grows with each additional wrong attempt.
- **Inactivity Timeout:** Configurable automatic relocking after inactivity, plus immediate locking upon backgrounding. The one exception is while a sign-in or unlock prompt the app itself opened is on screen: the system reports those the same way it reports you leaving, so locking is deferred for their duration. The app's contents stay masked throughout, the app relocks as soon as the prompt closes if you have left, and a prompt left open relocks the app after two minutes regardless of the inactivity setting.
- **Screen Obfuscation:** App switcher and lock screen previews are masked to prevent unauthorized viewing.
- **Transport Security:** All network transmissions use HTTPS with modern TLS (TLS 1.2/1.3) and strong cipher suites.
- **Cloud Storage Encryption (not end-to-end):** When cloud sync is enabled, your cycle entries are stored at rest in Supabase's infrastructure using AES-256 disk encryption — the same platform-level protection any well-run cloud database uses, applied by Supabase, not by an app-managed or end-to-end cipher LunarLog controls. LunarLog does not encrypt entry content client-side before it reaches Supabase: the note, tag, and flow values a guardian with database access could query are readable Postgres columns, protected by Row-Level Security (below) and Supabase's own infrastructure controls, not by a cipher only your devices hold the key to.
- **Database Row-Level Security:** Cloud database tables enforce PostgreSQL Row-Level Security (RLS) ensuring each authenticated user account can only read and write the rows their guardian memberships entitle them to; RLS governs which application queries can return which rows, not who can read the underlying data at the infrastructure or service-role level.

## 7. Data Export & Retention & Deletion Rights

You have complete control over your data:
- **Export Your Data:** From Settings → Your data, "Export my data" saves a JSON file of your profiles and day entries (built entirely from the copy already on your device, so it works fully offline and without a cloud account - it needs only that at least one profile exists on this device) and hands it to your device's normal share sheet - AirDrop, Files, email, or any app you choose. The file includes every profile and day entry already synced to this device - your own profiles, and, if you are an accepted caregiver or co-parent on someone else's profile (multi-guardian sharing, Settings → Account), that profile's entries too, since those are the same records you can already view and log in the app. It never includes sync-protocol internals, another account's authentication tokens, or credentials. It includes any same-date merge disclosures still inside their 30-day window (see Section 2.A) — a record that has aged past that window is gone from the app and never appears in an export. For a profile you own and share with other guardians, it does include each guardian's account identifier, role, and status — the same guardian list the app's Sharing screen already shows you, since that membership list belongs to a profile you own; for a profile shared with you rather than owned by you, the export includes only your own membership row, never a co-guardian's. When you are signed in and online, the file additionally merges in a section covering your account's own server-side data - see the Data Portability entry below for what that includes; signed out, or without a network connection, the export still completes using only what is on this device, and the file records that the server step did not run. If you are exporting on a shared or borrowed device, review the file before sharing it further - it reflects everything that account can currently see, not only entries you personally logged. A private day note (Section 5) is never part of a guardian's export: when the person exporting is not the note's subject, a note the subject marked private is omitted from the JSON and CSV output (the FHIR and PDF clinical summaries never include note text at all), and the subject's own export carries it in full.
- **Export a Clinical Summary (FHIR):** The same "Your data" section also offers "Export clinical summary (FHIR)" - a separate file, built entirely on-device the same way, in the FHIR R4 clinical-data format some doctors' offices and patient portals can import directly. It covers one profile's self-reported cycle and symptom history, coded using standard clinical terminologies (LOINC and SNOMED CT) where a verified mapping exists, and a lunarlog-specific code otherwise - never a guessed clinical code. It carries only that profile's display name (no email, no account id, no internal identifier of any kind) and is explicitly labeled throughout as self-reported by you or your family, not a clinician's assessment. This file is shaped like an international clinical-summary standard (the "International Patient Summary") without claiming full compliance with it, since a few sections that standard expects (allergies, medications) aren't things this app tracks yet.
- **Export as CSV:** The same "Your data" section also offers "Export as CSV" - two spreadsheet-compatible files, a per-cycle `cycles` table and a day-per-row `daily_log` table, built entirely on-device like the others. Unlike the JSON and FHIR files, the daily log carries your free-text notes and tags in their own columns, so treat a CSV file with the same care as "Export my data" - and it is subject to the same guardian-lens rule stated above, so a note the subject marked private is omitted whenever the exporter is not the subject. Every field is escaped against spreadsheet formula injection: any text starting with a formula trigger character (`=`, `+`, `-`, `@`, a tab, or a carriage return) is prefixed and quoted so a spreadsheet shows it as text rather than running it.
- **Export a Clinical Summary (PDF):** The same "Your data" section also offers "Export clinical summary (PDF)" - a one-page, on-device summary for a clinician, generated from the same self-reported cycle and symptom data. It carries only the profile's display name, no internal identifier, and never reads or includes note text.
- **Local Deletion:** You can delete individual cycle entries or entire profiles from the app at any time. Deleted records are tombstoned and permanently removed upon sync.
- **Sign Out & Local Wipe:** Signing out of your account gives you the option to discard all local database records from that device immediately.
- **Sign Out Everywhere:** You can invalidate sessions across all devices from the Account settings.
- **Account & Cloud Deletion:** From Settings → Account, "Delete account" immediately and permanently deletes your account: every server row you own (profiles, day entries, settings, your guardian memberships and invitations, your minimum-age acknowledgement record, and the small progress record kept for any bulk import you ran — a source label, row counts, status, and timestamps, no health content), the Supabase account itself (including, if you signed in with Apple, revoking that app's access via Apple's own revocation endpoint), and this device's local data. Your feedback tickets and their reply threads, and any attached screenshots, are also deleted explicitly as part of this same process (see "Support Ticket Retention" below) — not left to happen only as a side effect of the account being removed. The confirmation offers "Export first" so you can save a copy before proceeding. This is not a request queued for later - the server-side rows and the account are normally gone by the time the confirmation completes. The one exception: for an Apple-signed-in account, your data rows are deleted first and then, if Apple cannot confirm the revocation, the account sign-in itself is deliberately left in place rather than deleted with a live Apple grant still attached - the app tells you what happened and lets you retry, which completes the deletion. If any attached screenshot cannot be removed from storage, the same fail-closed rule applies: the deletion stops and is left retryable rather than completing with a screenshot left behind. You can still reach us at `will@wjdavis5.net` for any deletion the in-app flow does not cover (e.g. if you no longer have access to the device).
- **Support Ticket Retention:** Feedback tickets and their reply threads are retained to support ongoing conversations and app improvement; you can request deletion of a ticket at any time by emailing `will@wjdavis5.net`, and every ticket you own — along with any attached screenshot — is also removed automatically the moment you delete your account (see above): account deletion fails closed rather than completing with a ticket or screenshot left behind. Deleting a single ticket by emailing us (without deleting your whole account) removes the ticket and reply-thread database records themselves; an attached screenshot for that single-ticket case still needs your account to exist at request time so we can delete that file in the same pass, unlike the whole-account path above, which removes the screenshot itself as part of deleting the account. This narrower, request-only gap for a single ticket kept while your account remains open is a tracked improvement, not yet built. A resolved ticket that neither of the above ever cleans up is also aged out automatically after 1 year — see "Server-Side Retention Windows" immediately below.
- **Server-Side Retention Windows:** Beyond the rights above, a nightly automated process ages out five narrow categories of server-side record so nothing accumulates forever by default: a content-free caregiver-alert delivery record (which guardian was notified that an entry was logged, never the entry itself) is deleted 30 days after it was sent; a deleted cycle entry's server-side deletion marker — kept briefly so every one of your other synced devices has a chance to learn the entry was deleted, even one that has not opened the app in a while — is permanently removed 180 days after you deleted it, a window chosen to comfortably exceed how long a device can realistically stay offline before its next sync; a resolved support ticket **with no attached screenshot** (and its reply thread) is removed 1 year after it was marked resolved, on top of the manual and account-deletion paths described above — a resolved ticket that still has an attachment is left alone by this automatic process specifically because the attachment itself lives in separate storage this process cannot safely reach, and stays covered only by the "Support Ticket Retention" paths above (a request to us, or deleting your account) until it is deleted or its attachment is removed; and a shared per-entry change-history record — showing THAT a guardian's entry changed, who changed it, when, and which fields, never what they said — is removed 90 days after the change; and a same-date merge disclosure — the record that keeps a note or flow level that lost a merge between two guardians' entries for one date, so its author can recover it in the app — is removed 30 days after the merge, together with the discarded text it holds. The first four are delivery, sync, and support bookkeeping rather than health content on their own; the fifth is the one deliberate exception, holding a copy of health text for exactly as long as its author needs to see the in-app notice and re-enter it, and no longer. Each is a hard, permanent deletion, not a hidden extension of how long your actual cycle data is retained.
